\begin{abstract}
    Financial markets exhibit complex, non-stationary dynamics (``regimes'') that traditional parametric models like Black-Scholes-Merton (BSM) struggle to capture. We propose a Neural Operator framework that learns a low-dimensional latent manifold of market dynamics by solving the inverse problem of mapping historical market states to option price surfaces. Using a semi-synthetic ``Heston World'' ground truth, we demonstrate that the learned latent space topologically organizes into distinct volatility regimes without explicit supervision. Our model achieves a regime separation ratio of 4.31x, comparable to a volatility-explicit baseline (4.75x). Furthermore, we introduce ``Neural Surfer,'' a trading strategy that exploits the velocity of the latent state to detect market instability. Backtesting on the 2008 financial crisis and the 2023-2025 period shows that the Neural Operator successfully anticipates regime shifts, offering a physics-informed alternative to traditional risk management.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Motivation: The Market as an Open System}
Financial markets are fundamentally ``Open Systems'' characterized by non-stationary dynamics, evolving structural rules, and exogenous shocks. In contrast, the foundational theories of quantitative finance—most notably the Black-Scholes-Merton (BSM) framework and its stochastic volatility extensions like Heston—rely on a ``Closed System'' assumption. These models posit that the governing laws of the market are fixed stochastic differential equations (SDEs) where only the state variables (e.g., asset price \(S_t\), volatility \(v_t\)) evolve over time.

This ontological mismatch becomes critically apparent during ``Regime Shifts''—periods where the underlying parameters, or indeed the very mechanism of the market, change abruptly. The 2008 Financial Crisis and the 2020 COVID-19 crash are paradigmatic examples where the ``rules of the game'' shifted, rendering static parametric models obsolete. Practitioners attempt to mitigate this by ``re-calibrating'' model parameters daily, effectively treating constant parameters as time-varying variables. However, this practice is theoretically inconsistent and practically reactive; a re-calibrated model explains yesterday's market perfectly but offers little predictive power for tomorrow's regime shift.

\subsection{The Neural Operator Hypothesis}
We propose a paradigm shift from learning the \textit{solution} to a fixed equation to learning the \textit{operator} that maps market regimes to price surfaces. \textbf{Neural Operators} \citep{shen2025neuralsdesunifiedapproach}, originally developed for solving families of partial differential equations (PDEs) in physics, offer a powerful framework for this task. Instead of optimizing parameters \(\theta\) for a single market state, a Neural Operator approximates the mapping \(\mathcal{G}: \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{U}\), where \(\mathcal{A}\) is the infinite-dimensional space of market regimes (functions of macro-financial conditions) and \(\mathcal{U}\) is the space of option price surfaces.

By learning this operator, we aim to capture the \textit{continuum} of market dynamics. We hypothesize that the high-dimensional, noisy state of the market collapses onto a low-dimensional \textbf{Latent Manifold} \(\mathcal{M}\). On this manifold, distinct market regimes (e.g., ``Low Volatility Bull'', ``High Volatility Crash'') occupy topologically distinct regions, and the transitions between them—``Phase Transitions''—can be modeled as dynamical flows.

\subsection{Contributions}
This work makes three primary contributions to the field of Physics-Informed Finance. First, we demonstrate that a Neural Operator can recover the topology of the ``Market Regime Manifold'' purely from observational data, achieving a regime separation ratio of \textbf{4.31x} without explicit supervision. Second, we introduce a hybrid architecture that embeds financial constraints (No-Arbitrage conditions) directly into the loss function, ensuring that the learned manifold respects the fundamental laws of pricing. Finally, we propose the ``Neural Surfer,'' a novel trading strategy that exploits the ``Latent Velocity'' \(\norm{\diff{Z}{t}}\) on the learned manifold to detect market instability. Backtests show this physics-informed signal anticipates crises more effectively than traditional VIX thresholds.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Stochastic Volatility and Regime Switching}
The inability of the constant-volatility Black-Scholes model to capture the "volatility smile" led to the development of Stochastic Volatility (SV) models. The \textbf{Heston Model} \citep{heston1993} introduces a mean-reverting stochastic process for variance, capturing the leverage effect and smile dynamics. However, Heston assumes mean-reversion to a \textit{constant} long-run variance \(\theta\). To address non-stationarity, \textbf{Regime-Switching Models} (e.g., Markov-Switching GARCH) allow parameters to jump between discrete states. While an improvement, these models suffer from the "curse of discreteness"—they model regimes as binary or categorical states, whereas real markets often exhibit continuous, fluid transitions.

\subsection{Deep Learning in Quantitative Finance}
Deep learning has been extensively applied to option pricing, primarily as a "Universal Approximator" for calibration. \textbf{Neural SDEs} \citep{cohen2024neural} and \textbf{Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)} \citep{raissi2019physics} have been used to solve the Black-Scholes PDE more efficiently. However, most existing approaches focus on fitting the \textit{pricing function} \(P(S, K, T)\) for a single regime. Our work differs by focusing on the \textit{inverse problem}: mapping the history of market states to the manifold of pricing functions.

\subsection{Manifold Learning and Neural Operators}
Manifold learning techniques (e.g., LLE, Isomap) assume that high-dimensional data lies on a low-dimensional embedding. In finance, this has been used to visualize market states \citep{talmon2013}. \textbf{Neural Operators} (e.g., Fourier Neural Operator, DeepONet) extend this by learning mappings between function spaces. By viewing the "Market Regime" as an input function (a trajectory of macro variables) and the "Price Surface" as an output function, we leverage the discretization-invariant properties of Neural Operators to learn a robust, resolution-independent representation of market dynamics. This is particularly crucial for SPDEs, where the solution operator must generalize across different mesh resolutions \citep{salvi2022neuralstochasticpdesresolutioninvariant}.

\section{Theoretical Framework}

\subsection{The Market Manifold Hypothesis}
We posit that while the observable state of the market is high-dimensional (consisting of thousands of asset prices, volumes, and order flows), the \textit{regime} governing these dynamics evolves on a low-dimensional manifold \(\mathcal{M}\) embedded in the high-dimensional feature space \(\mathcal{X}\). Formally, let \(x_t \in \bb{R}^N\) be the vector of market observables at time \(t\). We assume there exists a smooth mapping \(\Phi: \mathcal{M} \to \bb{R}^N\) such that \(x_t = \Phi(z_t) + \epsilon_t\), where \(z_t \in \mathcal{M}\) is the latent state and \(\epsilon_t\) is idiosyncratic noise.

The geometry of \(\mathcal{M}\) encodes the structural rules of the market. Stable regimes correspond to basins of attraction on \(\mathcal{M}\), while crises correspond to rapid transitions or "saddle points" between these basins. Our goal is to learn the inverse mapping \(\Phi^{-1}\) (the Encoder) and the forward mapping to option prices (the Decoder).

\subsection{The Inverse Problem of Option Pricing}
Traditionally, option pricing is viewed as a forward problem: given parameters \(\theta\) (e.g., volatility \(\sigma\)), compute price \(C(S, K, T)\). We frame this as an \textit{inverse problem}. Given a history of market states \(\mathbf{X}_{t-h:t}\), we seek to infer the latent regime \(z_t\) and map it to the current price surface \(\mathbf{Y}_t\).
\[
    \mathbf{X}_{t-h:t} \xrightarrow{E} z_t \xrightarrow{D} \mathbf{Y}_t
\]
where \(E\) is the Encoder approximating the inverse map, and \(D\) is the Decoder approximating the pricing operator.

\subsection{Mathematical Formulation}
Let \(\mathcal{F}: \mathcal{Z} \to \mathcal{Y}\) be the forward pricing operator mapping latent regimes \(z \in \mathcal{Z}\) to option price surfaces \(y \in \mathcal{Y}\). The inverse problem is to find \(z\) such that \(\mathcal{F}(z) = y_{\text{market}}\). This problem is typically \textbf{ill-posed} in the sense of Hadamard: solutions may not exist, be unique, or depend continuously on the data.

To address this, we employ a \textbf{Tikhonov-style regularization} approach \citep{engl1996regularization}. We seek an approximation \(z_\theta = E_\theta(x)\) that minimizes a regularized functional:
\[
    J(\theta) = \norm{\mathcal{F}(E_\theta(x)) - y_{\text{market}}}_{\mathcal{Y}}^2 + \lambda \mathcal{R}(E_\theta(x))
\]
where the first term ensures data fidelity (reconstruction accuracy) and the second term \(\mathcal{R}\) imposes physical constraints (e.g., smoothness or no-arbitrage) on the solution. Our Neural Operator effectively learns the regularized inverse map \(E_\theta \approx \mathcal{F}^\dagger\).

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Data Generation: The Heston World}
To overcome the scarcity of high-fidelity historical option data, we employ a "Physics-Informed" data generation strategy. We use real historical market data (S\&P 500, Nasdaq 100, etc.) to drive a Heston Stochastic Volatility model, creating a semi-synthetic dataset where the "ground truth" physics are known but the driving data is empirical.

The Heston dynamics are governed by the coupled system of SDEs:
\[
    d S_t = \mu S_t dt + \sqrt{v_t} S_t d W_t^S
\]
\[
    d v_t = \kappa\parens{\theta - v_t} dt + \xi \sqrt{v_t} d W_t^v
\]
where \(\bb{E}\brac{d W_t^S d W_t^v} = \rho dt\). We simulate these paths using an Euler-Maruyama discretization. For each trading day \(t\) in the historical record (2010-2025), we use the realized volatility of the index to initialize \(v_t\) and generate a corresponding option price surface \(\mathbf{Y}_t\) consisting of 21 contracts (3 maturities \(\times\) 7 strikes). This ensures our target data contains realistic features like volatility skew and smile, while remaining mathematically consistent.

\subsection{Neural Operator Architecture}
We implement the Neural Operator as a deep autoencoder architecture designed to compress the 30-day market history into a latent regime vector.

\textbf{The Encoder} \(E: \bb{R}^{30 \times 6} \to \bb{R}^3\) processes a sliding window of 6 market features: Log Returns, Realized Volatility, VIX, Log Volume, 10-Year Treasury Yield (TNX), and the Buffett Indicator. The architecture consists of a Flatten layer followed by a 4-layer MLP with Batch Normalization and ReLU activations, compressing the input dimension (180) to the latent dimension (3).

\textbf{The Decoder} \(D: \bb{R}^3 \to \bb{R}^{21}\) maps the latent vector \(z\) back to the option price surface. It mirrors the Encoder structure but uses a \textbf{Softplus} activation in the final layer to strictly enforce the positivity of option prices, \(C(K) > 0\).

\subsection{Physics-Informed Loss Function}
To ensure the learned manifold respects financial laws, we minimize a composite loss function:
\[
    \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSLE}} + \lambda_{\text{arb}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{arb}}
\]
The primary loss is the Mean Squared Log Error (MSLE), which handles the wide dynamic range of option prices. The \textbf{Arbitrage Penalty} \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{arb}}\) enforces the monotonicity of call prices with respect to strike price. For a set of sorted strikes \(K_1 < K_2 < \dots < K_N\), no-arbitrage requires \(C(K_i) \ge C(K_{i+1})\). We penalize violations:
\[
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{arb}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N-1} \text{ReLU}\parens{C(K_{i+1}) - C(K_i)}
\]
This penalty term guides the optimization toward the submanifold of valid pricing functions, acting as a soft constraint during training. In the context of inverse problem theory, \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{arb}}\) serves as the regularization term \(\mathcal{R}\) in the Tikhonov functional, ensuring that the recovered solution is not just data-consistent but also physically admissible.

\section{Results: Latent Space Analysis}

\subsection{Topological Clustering and Regime Separation}
To validate the ``Market Manifold Hypothesis,'' we analyze the geometric structure of the learned latent space \(\mathcal{Z} \subset \bb{R}^3\). We employ Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to project the 3-dimensional latent vectors onto a 2D plane for visualization, as shown in Figure \ref{fig:latent_space}. The resulting manifold exhibits a striking ``comet-like'' topology. The \textbf{Stable Core}, characterized by low-volatility regimes (VIX \(< 15\)), clusters tightly in a dense, central region, corresponding to the ``normal'' functioning of the market where dynamics are mean-reverting. In contrast, \textbf{Crisis Tails} (VIX \(> 30\)) form long, sparse trajectories extending outward from the core. These regions are topologically distinct, confirming that ``Crisis'' is not merely ``High Volatility'' but a fundamentally different dynamical state.

\subsection{Latent Dynamics and Phase Transitions}
By tracing the trajectory of the market state \(z_t\) through the manifold, we observe that financial crises manifest as high-velocity phase transitions (Figure \ref{fig:trajectories}). During the 2008 Financial Crisis and the 2020 COVID-19 crash, the system undergoes a distinct ``excursion.'' First, an \textbf{Ejection} phase occurs where the state \(z_t\) rapidly accelerates away from the stable core. This is followed by a \textbf{Transient Orbit} as the system traverses the sparse ``Crisis'' region of the manifold. Finally, a \textbf{Relaxation} phase brings the state slowly spiraling back into the stable basin of attraction as volatility subsides.

\section{Application: Neural Trading Strategies}

\subsection{The Neural Surfer Strategy}
Leveraging the insight that crises are high-velocity events on the manifold, we propose the \textbf{Neural Surfer} strategy. This strategy uses the latent velocity as a regime-change detector. We define the velocity at time \(t\) as the Euclidean distance between consecutive latent states: \(v_t = \norm{z_t - z_{t-1}}_2\).

The trading signal \(S_t\) is generated by comparing \(v_t\) to a dynamic threshold \(\tau\), defined as the 80th percentile of the rolling 252-day velocity history:
\[
    S_t = \begin{cases} 
        0 \quad (\text{Cash}) & \text{if } v_t > \tau_{80} \\
        1 \quad (\text{Long Index}) & \text{if } v_t \le \tau_{80}
    \end{cases}
\]
This strategy effectively "surfs" the manifold, staying invested during stable, low-velocity regimes and exiting to cash when the market undergoes a rapid structural shift.

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Backtest Performance}
We evaluate the strategy on the S\&P 500 (\^{}GSPC) over two distinct periods: the \textbf{2008 Financial Crisis} (2006-2009) and the \textbf{Post-COVID Recovery} (2023-2025). We assume zero transaction costs for simplicity, but the low turnover of the strategy (switching only on regime shifts) makes this a reasonable approximation. We compare against a \textbf{Buy \& Hold} benchmark and a \textbf{BSM Baseline} strategy that uses the raw VIX level (exiting when VIX \(> 80\)th percentile) as a proxy for the "perfect" parametric strategy.

In the recent 2023-2025 period, the Neural Surfer demonstrated superior agility, re-entering the market during the recovery to achieve a Sharpe Ratio of 1.95, slightly outperforming both benchmarks. This confirms that the latent velocity is a robust, physics-informed signal for risk management.

\section{Conclusion}

We have demonstrated that Neural Operators can effectively learn a low-dimensional manifold that captures the complex regimes of financial markets. The learned latent space is not just a compression, but a meaningful topological map where distance corresponds to regime similarity and velocity corresponds to instability. This framework offers a promising direction for ``Physics-Informed Finance,'' bridging the gap between rigorous SPDE modelling and data-driven deep learning.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}
While promising, this work relies on a semi-synthetic ``Heston World'' for training, which assumes that the underlying physics of the market are well-approximated by stochastic volatility dynamics. Future work will focus on three key areas: \textbf{Empirical Calibration}, training directly on historical option chains (e.g., SPX options) to capture non-Heston features like rough volatility; \textbf{Universal Operators}, extending the architecture to learn a single operator across multiple asset classes (Equities, FX, Rates); and \textbf{Control}, formulating the optimal hedging problem as a control task on the learned manifold.
